# Classify Dhaka hospitals by name, not tags

The tag `amenity=hospital` in Dhaka's OpenStreetMap data cannot tell you whether a place has a 24-hour emergency department, so name keywords have to do most of the work. In a live Overpass snapshot of greater Dhaka (taken 21-22 Oct 2026), **482 elements carry a hospital tag, but only 42 carry any `emergency=*` value, none carry `beds`, and the set includes diagnostic centres, doctors' chambers, pharmacies, dental and veterinary practices.** Several of the 37 `emergency=yes` records are a homeopathic pharmacy, two dental clinics and an outpatient block ([Overpass API](https://overpass-api.de/api/interpreter)). The best approach is a layered classifier. First, drop non-hospital service words (diagnostic, chamber, pharmacy, homeo, dental, physio, vet). Second, promote government tiers that reliably run round-the-clock emergency care (Medical College Hospital, General Hospital, Upazila Health Complex). Third, label specialised institutes (eye, trauma, child, burn, heart) as "specialised-only". Fourth, treat ordinary private "Hospital" and "Clinic" names as "likely", with low confidence, because Bangladeshi law puts them in the same category and enforcement drives keep finding emergency units that exist "only in name" ([Dhaka Tribune](https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/health/413765/not-just-ad-din-dhaka%E2%80%99s-healthcare-death-traps)). Strong sources confirm 24-hour emergency service at only a few Dhaka facilities: Dhaka Medical College Hospital, Bangladesh Medical University (formerly BSMMU), NITOR, the National Institute of Ophthalmology and Evercare. Many others are near-certain in practice but rest on weak sources. For hotlines, **999** is the number to feature. **102** has replaced 16163 for the fire service. The DGHS doctor-advice line **16263** faced a funding shutdown in early 2026, and no source shows what happened after that.

## OSM's hospital tag claims inpatient care, but Dhaka mappers apply it to almost anything medical

The global convention is clear. `amenity=hospital` means "a hospital providing in-patient medical treatment". Outpatient centres with roughly ten or more staff belong under `amenity=clinic`, and small practices under `amenity=doctors` ([OSM wiki: amenity=hospital](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Tag:amenity=hospital); [OSM wiki: amenity=clinic](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Tag:amenity=clinic)). `healthcare=hospital` is an intended duplicate. A proposed migration to it is still disputed, so consumers must read both keys. Labs, imaging, sample collection, pharmacies, physiotherapists, blood banks, dialysis and rehabilitation all have their own `healthcare=*` values ([OSM wiki: Key:healthcare](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:healthcare)). Four optional attributes bear directly on emergency triage. **`emergency=yes` on a hospital means it is "equipped to deal with emergencies"** ([OSM wiki: Key:emergency](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:emergency)). The hospital page asks mappers to add yes or no if known, and to mark the ward door with an `emergency=emergency_ward_entrance` node. Second, `healthcare:speciality` takes semicolon-separated British-spelling values such as `emergency`, `trauma`, `paediatrics`, `ophthalmology` and `cardiology`. The wiki documents no `burns` or general `dentistry` value ([OSM wiki: healthcare:speciality](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:healthcare:speciality)). Third, `beds` is an integer count ([OSM wiki: Key:beds](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:beds)). Fourth, `operator:type` distinguishes private, public, government, ngo, religious and similar values, though the wiki itself calls the public/government split "confusingly defined" ([OSM wiki: operator:type](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:operator:type)).

Dhaka's data falls far short of this. The Overpass snapshot covered the bbox 23.65,90.30 to 23.92,90.52 (Keraniganj to Uttara). It returned **482 elements: 478 with `amenity=hospital`, 316 with `healthcare=hospital` (312 with both), 30 with no name at all, 0 with `beds`, 15 with `operator:type`, 19 with `healthcare:speciality`, and 14 with `opening_hours=24/7`** ([Overpass API](https://overpass-api.de/api/interpreter)). That leaves about **91% of hospital records with no emergency information**. The emergency tag that does exist is noisy. Genuine emergency hospitals carry `emergency=yes`: Dhaka Medical College Hospital, Mugda Medical College Hospital, Square, Dhaka Shishu, Ibn Sina, Labaid Cardiac and Farazy. So do "Meherun Homeo Pharmacy", "Tooth Fairy", "Sazzad's Amazing Dental", "Aalok Diagnostic Center" and "PG Outdoor", which is an outpatient unit. The five `emergency=no` records look more trustworthy: they include a TB hospital and Prime Bank Eye Hospital. Al Razi Hospital Complex is also among them, which is surprising and should be checked.

Pollution is the larger problem. Among names under `amenity=hospital` there are **22 containing "diagnos", 10 doctors' chambers, 5 pharmacies, 9 dental practices**, plus homeopathy, hijama (cupping), veterinary, nursing-college and hospital sub-unit records such as "Cabin Block" and "BSMMU Outdoor 1 (Medicine)". Another 56 names contain no facility word at all. Three records contradict themselves by pairing `amenity=hospital` with `healthcare=physiotherapist`, `occupational_therapist` or `laboratory`. Addresses leak into names: **104 names contain a comma**, and ten follow the Google Maps listing pattern "<Name>, Hospital, <Road>, Dhaka", such as "LabAid Limited (Diagnostic), Hospital, Doyagonj New Rd, Dhaka". Duplicates are common. Point-plus-polygon pairs exist for EverCare, AMZ and Japan Bangladesh Friendship Hospital. NITOR appears as three ways. Variant spellings such as "Asgar Ali" and "Ali Asgar" show up as separate records. One private hospital is tagged `operator:type=public` with `operator=xyz` ([Overpass API](https://overpass-api.de/api/interpreter)). These are counts from raw OSM. Geoapify's `healthcare.hospital` category is built from the same data but may filter or merge records, which this research did not check.

Language is the final complication. In the snapshot, **77 names are in Bengali script, 20 use the form "English(বাংলা)", and only 78 have `name:en` and 40 have `name:bn`**. Romanised Bangla also appears, as in "Maula Buksh Sardar Datobbo Chokkhu Hospital" ([Overpass API](https://overpass-api.de/api/interpreter)). The confusion has roots in the community. In a 2019 talk-bd debate, Bangladeshi mappers said they had put English in `name` for more than four years. Others argued for Bangla in `name` with `name:en` alongside, as global OSM guidance recommends ([talk-bd, Nov 2019](https://lists.openstreetmap.org/pipermail/talk-bd/2019-November/000152.html)). Third-party datasets do not fix this. Healthsites.io writes edits straight into OSM and mirrors OSM changes back ([OSM wiki: Global Healthsites Mapping Project](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Global_Healthsites_Mapping_Project)), and HOT's Bangladesh health-facility export on HDX is another OSM snapshot ([HDX](https://data.humdata.org/m/dataset/hotosm_bgd_health_facilities)). Neither is an independent register. A 2019 PLOS ONE study of 24/7 emergency access for Dhaka's urban poor ran into the same problem: there was no capacity data for health facilities ([PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0222488)).

The table below shows how each OSM signal should count in the classifier.

| OSM signal | How to use it | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `amenity=hospital` / `healthcare=hospital` | Gets a place into the candidate set; does not prove anything on its own | Dhaka applies it to diagnostics, chambers, pharmacies, vets |
| `emergency=no` | Strong negative; exclude from general ED results | Few records, and most look accurate (TB, eye) |
| `emergency=yes` | Weak positive; the name filter must still pass | Several false positives (homeo pharmacy, dental, diagnostic, OPD) |
| `opening_hours=24/7` | Weak positive | Only 14 records; says nothing about whether there is an ED |
| `healthcare=` laboratory / physiotherapist / other non-hospital value alongside `amenity=hospital` | Reclassify to the `healthcare` value; no ED | The tags contradict each other, and the specific value is more credible |
| `healthcare:speciality` | Use for a speciality badge, not for gating; normalise US/UK spellings and the non-standard values found (`dentistry`, `neunatal`, `veterinary`) | Sparse and partly non-standard in Dhaka |
| `beds`, `operator:type` | Ignore for now | 0 and 15 records, and the operator tags are partly wrong |
| Missing tags | "Unknown", not "no" | Every attribute is optional |

## Bangladeshi law makes "hospital" and "clinic" one category, so the name tells you the licence but not the quality

The naming conventions follow a legal split. The Medical Practice and Private Clinics and Laboratories (Regulation) Ordinance, 1982 defines a "private clinic" as **"a clinic, hospital or nursing home, by whatever name called"** where patients are admitted. It defines private laboratories (tests, X-ray, ECG) separately ([bdlaws, Ordinance 1982 s.2](https://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-print-620/section-print-11782.html)). The DGHS Hospital and Clinic branch issues separate licences and keeps separate lists for hospitals/clinics, diagnostic centres (Categories A/B/C), blood banks, ICU/CCU and dialysis centres ([DGHS Hospital & Clinic Section](http://hospitaldghs.gov.bd/private-facilities-registration/)). DGHS directives issued in February 2024 bar a facility branded "diagnostic and hospital" from offering the other service if it holds only one of the two licences ([UNB](https://unb.com.bd/category/Bangladesh/dghs-drive-37-unregistered-hospitals-sealed-across-the-country/93796)). A public-health expert notes that Bangladesh is unusual in licensing diagnostic centres separately from hospitals ([TBS](https://www.tbsnews.net/features/panorama/no-other-country-are-hospitals-and-diagnostic-centres-separate-entities-368407)). This explains the familiar pairing of "X Diagnostic Centre" and "X Hospital" under the same brand. The token in the name tells you which licence, and therefore whether inpatient or emergency care, applies. Since December 2022, doctors' chambers may operate only inside registered hospitals, clinics or diagnostic centres ([The Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/health/news/doctors-must-run-private-practices-registered-facilities-dghs-3189186)). A chamber is therefore never an emergency destination in its own right. Homeopathy and Unani/Ayurveda fall under entirely separate laws ([bdlaws Act 1482](https://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-1482.html)).

The scale shows why private-sector names deserve low confidence. **Dhaka district alone has 827 registered private hospitals and clinics, 1,544 diagnostic centres and 152 blood banks.** Inspections found hospitals of 13 to 27 beds with an "emergency" room and no doctor on duty ([Dhaka Tribune](https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/health/413765/not-just-ad-din-dhaka%E2%80%99s-healthcare-death-traps)). A 2024 DGHS drive penalised "Authentic Hospital" and "Ahmed Specialised Hospital" partly for having no emergency department ([The Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/news/bangladesh/news/dghs-drive-opens-can-worms-4135766)), so the word "Specialised" in a private name guarantees nothing. Bangladesh Specialized Hospital in Shyamoli, by contrast, advertises a 24/7 emergency with round-the-clock CT and MRI ([hospital site](https://bdspecializedhospital.com/department/emergency)). The public system is more predictable. Upazila Health Complexes, usually 50-bed hospitals, provide inpatient, outpatient and emergency care ([BMJ Open via PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8424828/table/T2)). Medical college hospitals and national institutes form the tertiary tier ([Wikipedia: Health in Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_in_Bangladesh)). Maternal and Child Welfare Centres provide emergency obstetric care, and some run 24 hours ([Ittefaq](https://en.ittefaq.com.bd/11333/health-centers-ensure-24-7-safe-delivery-services)).

Precedence decides most real cases. A service word that means No should override "Medical" and "Centre", but not an explicit "Hospital" or "হাসপাতাল" token. So "Ibn Sina Diagnostic" is No, "Ibn Sina Hospital" is Likely, and "X Hospital & Diagnostic Centre" is a hospital. A speciality word should override a generic "Hospital" and produce a specialised-only badge. This research gives this ordering as an inference. No source prescribes it. Normalisation must handle the ্য-phala (মেডিকেল vs মেডিক্যাল), হাসপাতাল vs হসপিটাল, Centre/Center, Specialised/Specialized, Paediatric/Pediatric, Orthopaedic/Orthopedic, Homoeo/Homeo, and typos such as "Diagonstic" and "Humiopathik" that appear in the live data ([Overpass API](https://overpass-api.de/api/interpreter)). Before matching, strip comma-separated address tails and split "English(বাংলা)" names into their two parts. Deduplicate by normalised name plus distance, for example within 150 m, and keep the polygon. Normalise Bengali names with a Unicode-aware method: an ASCII-only normaliser collapses every Bengali name into one key.

## Strong sources confirm only a handful of Dhaka emergency departments

No official DGHS list of 24-hour emergency facilities in Dhaka was found. The list below is a validation set for the classifier, not a hardcoded registry. Accident and emergency patients in Dhaka are "almost invariably forwarded" to government hospitals, and DMCH and NITOR are named as overburdened ([TBS](https://www.tbsnews.net/node/551766)). DGHS has reportedly told private hospitals to provide 24/7 emergency care under registered doctors. That comes from a news summary, and the circular itself was not seen ([Jago News 24](https://www.jagonews24.com/en/national/news/83286)). History matters for BMU. **In 2017 BSMMU had no general emergency department and sent patients to DMCH** ([The Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/backpage/lone-medical-university-running-without-emergency-unit-inception-1998-1384945)). It opened a 100-bed round-the-clock emergency unit only in November 2021 ([Financial Express](https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/home/bsmmu-launches-100-bed-emergency-unit-1635762854)). NIOH's emergency service was **shut from about 28 May to 4 June 2025** after violence and returned fully around 14 June ([Prothom Alo](https://en.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/city/12hyoo63ig); [Dhaka Tribune](https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/dhaka/383782/national-institute-of-ophthalmology-fully)). A 24/7 label can therefore lapse.

Aggregators warn that staffing can thin out after midnight and on public holidays even where 24/7 is claimed ([Medexly](https://medexly.com/emergency-hospitals-in-dhaka/)). During Eid, DMCH reported a shortage of specialists on site ([Jago News 24](https://www.jagonews24.com/en/national/news/83286)). "24-hour emergency" should therefore read as "a door that stays open", not "a full specialist team". Foreign missions also list Praava Health (Banani) and RA Hospital (Gulshan) as emergency options, without endorsing them ([Australian High Commission](https://bangladesh.embassy.gov.au/files/daca/09102024%20Information%20for%20Australians%20in%20Bangladesh%20-%20Updated%2030%20September%202024.pdf)).

Renames after 2024 make alias matching mandatory. On **3 Nov 2024** the health ministry renamed 14 institutions. Two are in Dhaka: the Burn Institute and the Gastro Liver Institute, both of which dropped Sheikh-family names ([Dhaka Tribune](https://www.dhakatribune.com/amp/bangladesh/government-affairs/364054/govt-changes-names-of-14-hospitals-including)). BSMMU became **Bangladesh Medical University** by a 2025 ordinance. Sources disagree on the date (13 March or 13 April 2025), and the Gazette has not been checked ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Medical_University); [bdnews24](https://bdnews24.com/bangladesh/4dd003f79e60)). OSM still carries "BSMMU Outdoor", "PG Outdoor" and "পিজি হাসপাতাল" ([Overpass API](https://overpass-api.de/api/interpreter)). The alias list should map BSMMU, Bangabandhu Sheikh Mujib Medical University and PG Hospital to BMU. It should also map Sheikh Hasina Burn to the Burn Institute, Sheikh Russel Gastroliver to the Gastro Liver Institute, Dhaka Shishu to Bangladesh Shishu Hospital & Institute, and Apollo Dhaka to Evercare. Classify by the speciality keyword, not the honorific. Other Sheikh-named units, such as the Sheikh Fazilatunnesa Mujib facilities, were not checked.

## 999 is the only hotline the app can show without caveats

**999 is Bangladesh's single toll-free, 24/7 emergency number.** Bangladesh Police run it from the Police Control Room in Dhaka, and it routes calls to police, fire and ambulance. Between Dec 2017 and Apr 2026 it handled **71.17 million calls, of which 55.4% needed no service**. Ambulance dispatches numbered 225,611 ([Dhaka Tribune, May 2026](https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/government-affairs/410362/fake-unnecessary-calls-delay-999-emergency)). The call is free but the ambulance is not. A government portal says no authority provides free ambulance service ([bangladesh.gov.bd union portal](https://patanusharup.moulvibazar.gov.bd/en/site/page/npQv-%E0%A6%AB%E0%A7%8B%E0%A6%A8%E0%A7%87-%E0%A6%AA%E0%A7%81%E0%A6%B2%E0%A6%BF%E0%A6%B6%E0%A6%BF-%E0%A6%B8%E0%A6%B9%E0%A6%BE%E0%A7%9F%E0%A6%A4%E0%A6%BE)). Dhaka North City Corporation's five ambulances are dispatched through 999 ([Dhaka Tribune, May 2025](https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/dhaka/381020/dncc-inaugurates-heatstroke-centre)). No separate city-corporation ambulance number should be shown. The fire service moved to **102** in June 2024, and the older 16163 was due to end after 31 Dec 2024 ([TBS](https://www.tbsnews.net/bangladesh/fire-service-launches-3-digit-hotline-number-102-870521)). **16263 (Shastho Batayon)** is DGHS's free 24/7 doctor-advice line, with about 100 MBBS doctors and 2.35 million calls in 2025 ([Prothom Alo, Jan 2026](https://en.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/gdmucoatn3)). In February 2026 the operator had gone **17 months unpaid (Tk 11.24 crore)**, with the contract ending in April 2026 ([Observer](https://www.observerbd.com/news/565532); [Financial Express](https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/health/natl-telehealth-helpline-16263-faces-shutdown-amid-funding-crisis)).

Several points remain unverified. No source confirmed that any of these numbers works with zero balance or without a SIM, so the app should say "toll-free" rather than "works with no credit". fireservice.gov.bd could not be loaded, so neither the end of 16163 nor T&T coverage of 102 was confirmed on the official site. Old hospital ambulance numbers, such as a 2020 DMCH line, and DNCC's COVID-era relief lines were deliberately left out. Awareness is a product problem in its own right: in one survey **only 12% of women knew of 109, against 45% for 999** ([The Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/supplements/16-days-activism-2025/news/when-danger-strikes-where-can-women-turn-4042526)). An app that puts 999 first and labels it clearly adds real value.

## Conclusion

The main lesson is that "has a 24-hour emergency department" in Dhaka depends on the institution far more than on the map tag. The facilities that matter most are the few dozen government medical college hospitals, national institutes and major private hospitals, and they are few enough to keep in a curated alias table. Name rules can then handle the long tail of more than 1,500 diagnostic centres and hundreds of small clinics, mostly by excluding them. A practical design keeps four outputs: a confirmed or likely general ED, a specialised-only badge (trauma, eye, child, burn, heart, maternity), "unknown" for small private hospitals and clinics, and exclusion for non-hospitals. Confidence should come from the source (official, news, aggregator, OSM tag), not from OSM's `emergency=yes`, which in Dhaka is as likely to sit on a dental clinic as on DMCH.

Two maintenance obligations follow. Names and statuses drift: political renames in 2024-25, a week-long eye-hospital closure in 2025, and a 2026 funding crisis on 16263. Any "24/7" claim and any hotline therefore needs a last-verified date in the app, and a periodic check against the DGHS Facility Registry and official hospital pages. The unconfirmed rows also need work. Kurmitola, Kuwait-Bangladesh Friendship, the Burn Institute, NICVD and the large private hospitals are almost certainly open around the clock, and a short phone-verification pass would turn the "likely" tier into ground truth. Correcting the tags upstream in OSM would let every other Dhaka health app benefit too.
